Intake Assessment

Dropped calls are actively hurting Local Service Ads performance today, and Immigration's low conversion rate means the firm needs far more lead volume than it should to hit its own goals. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Dropped calls are actively hurting your Local Service Ads performance right now — a paid channel you're already spending money on
  • No after-hours or overflow coverage was described for either practice area
  • Immigration's retainer conversion rate sits at 5%, meaning most of the leads you already generate never convert
  • No defined follow-up process was described for leads who don't sign on the first call
Blind Spot: Every dropped call isn't just a missed conversation — it's a signal to Google that your LSA listing is less responsive, which can quietly reduce how often your ads show at all.
The Gap
  • At a 5% Immigration conversion rate, hitting your 50-signed-client goal requires roughly 1,000 leads a month — nearly double what would be needed at a more typical conversion rate
  • Every dropped call is a lead you already paid for, gone before it ever had a chance to convert
  • Fixing intake is the fastest path to more signed clients without spending another ad dollar
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Team Assessment

Marketing for 6 offices and 45 employees currently runs through one in-house person, and no dedicated ops or intake leadership was described on the call. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Marketing is run entirely by one person (Fred G.) across all 6 offices, with no dedicated marketing team supporting him
  • No dedicated ops, intake, or accountability structure was described for the dropped-call problem
  • No headcount breakdown beyond the 45-employee, 6-office total was given on the call
  • Two practice areas with very different economics — First-Party Property and Immigration — are being managed without apparent role specialization between them
Blind Spot: Fred G. being "open to outsourcing for better results" is itself the blind spot — it means the firm already knows the current structure has hit its ceiling, before the numbers even confirm it.
The Positive
  • The firm has already built real scale — 45 employees and 6 offices is a platform most firms never reach
  • Fred G. has explicitly said he's open to outsourcing for better results, a receptiveness to change that makes this an easier fix than at firms where the in-house owner resists letting go
The Gap
  • One person managing marketing across 6 offices means no one is dedicated to fixing what's already broken while also running day-to-day campaigns
  • Without a leadership layer between the owner and daily operations, decisions bottleneck through the same one or two people no matter how much the firm grows
  • Time that should go to firm strategy is instead going to marketing tasks a specialized team could run more effectively
Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Profit Plan Assessment

Beyond Immigration's own channel-level numbers, no revenue figure, cost-per-acquisition, or firm-wide profitability discussion was raised on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No firm-wide revenue figure was discussed on the call
  • No cost-per-acquisition visibility exists for First-Party Property, only for Immigration
  • The ad spend increase currently being discussed is well below what the firm's own math shows is needed to hit its stated goals
  • No practice-area-level profitability comparison was raised, despite First-Party Property and Immigration having very different economics
Blind Spot: Without knowing acquisition cost for First-Party Property specifically, it's impossible to know whether the next marketing dollar is better spent rebuilding that practice's lead volume or scaling Immigration's already-proven $82 CPL performance.
The Positive
  • Immigration already has real, measurable channel-level data — 187 leads, ~$82 CPL, 5% conversion, ~$1,640 CPA — proof the firm can track unit economics when it chooses to
The Gap
  • Without firm-wide financial visibility, it's difficult to know whether increased ad spend is actually improving the bottom line or just increasing top-line activity
  • The gap between what's currently being discussed and the spend required to hit stated volume goals cannot be closed without a clear view of what the firm can actually afford
  • Decisions about where to invest the next marketing dollar are being made without the data to compare practice areas against each other
Real financial visibility is what turns "scale and dominate" from a slogan into a plan you can actually execute and measure.
